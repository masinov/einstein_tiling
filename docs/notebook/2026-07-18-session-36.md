## The density-gap formulation

Write `k=[Lambda:L]`. The quotient has `6k` kite cells, so an exact cover by
the area-ten finalist needs exactly `3k/5` placements. Remove coverage
entirely and retain only:

1. one V4 signature's affine boundary-potential equations; and
2. the single forbidden D6 orbit of six-kite collisions.

Exact MaxSAT on three controls gives:

| HNF | k | maximum selected | k/2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `(2,0,2)` | 4 | 2 | 2 |
| `(4,0,2)` | 8 | 4 | 4 |
| `(2,0,10)` | 20 | 10 | 10 |

If `selected <= k/2` holds for every area-admissible `L <= 2 Lambda`, then
`k/2 < 3k/5` gives an immediate contradiction. This is the candidate T2.D7
density theorem. It is strictly stronger than the 193 exact-cover searches
and is not yet proved.

The two binary coordinates are both essential. Retaining either coordinate
alone gives maxima `k`, not `k/2`, on all three controls.

## Falsified easy proofs

Three tempting simplifications fail:

- **Independent 2x2 blocks:** an isolated 2x2 anchor block supports five
  placements, not two.
- **Pairwise conflict graph:** after eliminating potentials pairwise, the
  independence number is `k/2` on the tiny `k=4,8` controls, but the graph
  admits 9 placements on a 4x4 torus and 19 on a 6x6 torus. Pairwise packing
  is insufficient.
- **Only ternary gluing circuits:** the 4x4 nine-placement witness contains a
  minimal incompatible triple, but lazy core learning also finds genuine
  minimal affine circuits of sizes 4 and 5.

The crucial obstruction is therefore global consistency of a shared V4
potential. In sheaf language, locally compatible placement sections can have
nonzero holonomy around a cycle. Algebraically, the bad placement sets are
circuits of a binary affine representable matroid. This is precisely the
non-pairwise gluing layer that the earlier packing graph omitted.
