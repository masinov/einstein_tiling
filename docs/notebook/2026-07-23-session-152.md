## Done

Launched the two-batch HC-34 supervisor through the passing session-151
research gate from stable formula revision `8cbbd02`.  The launch failed
immediately.  Two cell logs contain only a pre-solver exception:

```text
RuntimeError: formula drift for <cell>; cold serialization no longer matches
```

The first cell had the same construction position as the serializer, passed
that check and entered Z3, but the enclosing screen died.  No solver,
`timeout` or research-wrapper process survived, and no cell result or
aggregate result manifest was created.

The defect is exact and non-mathematical.  The serializer constructed all six
cells sequentially.  The cell runner reconstructed one cell in a fresh
process with a timeout parameter.  Z3's generated `let` identifiers depend on
construction history, so semantically equivalent formulas can have different
SMT-LIB bytes.  Comparing regenerated text to a frozen artifact was therefore
the wrong provenance check.

The single-cell runner now:

1. loads the frozen formula manifest;
2. authenticates cell order, path, byte count and SHA-256;
3. parses the frozen SMT-LIB file directly into a QF_NRA solver;
4. verifies that the parsed solver has exactly 187 assertions; and
5. attaches the unchanged three-hour timeout and 32-GiB parameter.

No formula, constraint, cell, ordering or resource parameter changed.

## Verified

The byte mismatch reproduces without solving.  The first isolated build and
the sequential serializer can share a hash by accident, while later cells
receive different generated `let` names.  A regression test now imports the
runner and confirms it parses the pinned first formula as 187 assertions from
the recorded SHA-256 instead of calling the formula builder.

Targeted tests pass (`10 passed`) and both runner files compile.  The complete
suite passes: `232 passed, 17 deselected` in 147.71 seconds.

## Failure and evidence boundary

There is no SAT, UNSAT or `unknown` verdict.  The empty/exception logs are
launch diagnostics only.  In particular, the killed first process is not an
external three-hour resource stop and says nothing about feasibility.

Session 151's stop rule nevertheless states that a killed process leaves the
cell open and does not authorize a rerun.  The failed launch therefore closes
HC-34 without a decision.  All six frozen cells remain open.  A new explicit
human checkpoint is required to retry the identical files through the
repaired runner.

## Decision

D-0174 records the wrapper defect, the exact repair and the fail-closed retry
boundary.  Do not relaunch under HC-34.
