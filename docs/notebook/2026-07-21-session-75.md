## Question

Does any nontrivial quotient satisfying K1T exist without erasing the
addressed state irrecoverably, and what must a later geometry enforce?

## K1D

Encode each essential addressed source state `a` by an injective triple
`(c_0(a),c_1(a),c_2(a))`. Put one coordinate on each directed side contact.
No single contact retains the source identity. A legal tile star must form
one complete codeword; decoded neighboring and vertex states must satisfy the
source rules.

The three modes decode `a` uniquely, and re-encoding reproduces the quotient
configuration. Therefore this is a nontrivial K1T-safe quotient on its full
local-rule space. It redistributes information rather than minimizing the
number of legal source star types.

For `n` essential source states, an immediate three-contact decoder needs at
least `ceil(n^(1/3))` modes per directed side in a balanced code. Positive
large/small coefficients guarantee one 30-address large macro plus the
2-address small macro, so at least 32 essential states already force four
modes. The stronger 62-state count would require both large types to be
essential and is not assumed.

## N4 and decision

Independent side rules admit a Cartesian product of side modes. They cannot
enforce a non-Cartesian source codeword set, so they introduce spurious mixed
tile stars. A later carrier needs a genuine corner or tile-star coupler.

The symbolic K1 question is therefore feasible and closed in proof draft by
K1D. The next blocker is K2G: realize those coupled contact modes with one
fixed unmarked polygon while excluding illegal mixed stars, sliding,
T-junctions and opposite-handed faults. No such geometry is constructed.

HC-06 is exhausted after this session. K2G requires explicit authorization
under a new checkpoint.
