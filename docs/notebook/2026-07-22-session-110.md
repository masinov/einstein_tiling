# Session 110 — convex curvature and the point-plug no-go

**Date:** 2026-07-22

**Checkpoint:** `HC-2026-07-22-18`, session 2 of 3

**Mode:** on-paper curvature and local topology; no experiment

## Proposition

Determine whether K9A can be realized by adding one independent convex guard
corner, and whether the fourth occurrence can remain local to the primary
point without further contact structure.

## Prior art and non-redundancy

N21 treats complementary three-participant code ports and does not apply to
K9A's non-complementary four-sector equations. N23 treats an endpoint of one
two-tile interface. The present claims combine the convex turn budget across
both mandatory code roles and apply N23 to both interfaces created at the new
primary star. No general novelty claim is made.

## Result

K9V is a sharp curvature filter. Each of code sides `B,C` has endpoint turns
summing to `pi+gamma`, so they must share a polygon vertex. If its interior
angle is `alpha`, every other vertex receives total exterior turn
`pi-alpha-2*gamma`. A distinct guard corner of angle `gamma` cannot fit in
that remainder. The convex route survives only by literally reusing the
guard vertex on the `B/C` block; otherwise the carrier must be nonconvex.

N24 proves that a positive guard is not a point plug. Its two incident
interfaces have maximal arcs, and each arc must terminate at another
multi-tile junction. Thus the added sector necessarily has a residual contact
topology.

## Outcome decision and stop rule

The route is narrowed but not killed. Session 111 may retain it only by
giving a finite contact topology that accounts for the complete guard
boundary and keeps the `ABC/ACB` state visible. A picture, a dangling
interface, or an unnamed completion fires the stop.
