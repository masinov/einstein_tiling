# Research-return audit after the polykite prior-art correction

**Date:** 2026-07-21
**Purpose:** decide what the repository can honestly contribute before any
new experiment is admitted.

## Executive decision

The project built a strong exact-computation laboratory but allowed finite
controls to become the research agenda. The `n<=16` funnel lies entirely
inside the Hat paper's exhaustive `n<=24` classification. The promoted
ten-kite finalist is the published Turtle. The A2 eight-kite Heesch counts
exactly reproduce Kaplan's public artifact. The W2 quotient campaigns and W3
Spectre radius campaigns concern known tiles with published aperiodicity or
hierarchy proofs.

Accordingly:

- all radius/index/census escalation is frozen;
- no result below is called novel without a targeted primary-source audit;
- the next research action is a theorem-level prior-art audit, not a run;
- controls are retained because they support reproducibility and method
  evaluation, not because accumulating them approaches an aperiodicity proof.

## Keep and package

### 1. Exact verification infrastructure

The exact kite geometry, canonicalization, three-valued verdicts, independent
certificate verifiers, tamper tests, and external-anchor discipline are a
coherent reusable laboratory. This is primarily an engineering contribution.
Its public value depends on documentation, stable schemas, small reproducible
fixtures, and comparison with existing packages—not on retaining every large
proof artifact.

### 2. A classified-corpus benchmark

The funnel independently ranks the Hat first among eight-kites and later
surfaces the Turtle. A1+A2 exactly reproduce Myers/Kaplan aggregates. This can
become a legitimate benchmark or postmortem if it reports:

- the frozen candidate corpus and concealed labels;
- the information available to each stage;
- rank, false-positive count, runtime, and ablations;
- exact prior-art identities revealed only at evaluation;
- failure analysis, including the late Turtle identity check.

This would evaluate a discovery method. It would not discover a tile or
reclassify the corpus.

### 3. T2.C1/T2.C5 as a theorem candidate

The parity construction excludes three infinite thin HNF families for the
Turtle, using an explicit odd-weight support and exact dihedral transport.
Unlike a larger finite quotient prefix, this is an actual uniform statement.
It is still labelled **proof draft** and is about a known tile. Before further
development it needs a focused prior-art comparison against:

- classical coloring and Conway--Lagarias group invariants;
- the Hat/Turtle alignment and incommensurability arguments;
- existing algebraic exact-cover obstructions on quotient lattices;
- published Turtle Sturmian/Ammann-bar proofs.

Only if the obstruction or its certificate form is absent from that literature
should it be polished into a short theorem package. Otherwise it becomes a
worked control example.

## Retain as controls, not research claims

| work | honest status | disposition |
|---|---|---|
| A0 enumeration through `n=16` | independent enumeration inside a classified range | freeze; use only as a labeled corpus |
| A1 bounded torus search | exact bounded periodicity filter | retain small tests; no larger Turtle shells |
| A2 Heesch census through `n=8` | exact Kaplan reproduction | package benchmark; no novelty claim |
| A3 large disks/nested cores | finite continuability evidence | retain demonstrations; do not infer plane tilability |
| A4 diffraction | calibrated prioritization heuristic | retain E4 control; no proof use |
| W2 index `40..60` exclusions | exact finite theorems for known Turtle | archive/freeze; no index escalation |
| W3 Spectre local languages | partial reconstruction of a published hierarchy | freeze pending a concrete method-novelty proposition |

## Retire or suspend

1. Discovery claims anywhere in the polykite range `n<=24`.
2. Mechanical continuation at `n=25` or beyond without a source-backed reason
   to expect a qualitatively new mechanism.
3. Turtle quotient-index escalation and Spectre corona-radius escalation.
4. Any attempt to prove a known tile aperiodic by accumulating finite
   exclusions.
5. Re-proving standard compactness, generic recognizability, or classical
   period descent unless a genuinely new hypothesis or effective bound is
   identified first.

## Next decision sequence

1. Audit T2.C1/T2.C5 against primary algebraic-tiling literature. This is
   reading and proof comparison only.
2. If it survives, reduce it to a self-contained theorem, proof, and tiny
   verifier. If it does not, mark it as a control and stop that branch.
3. Independently assess whether the blind Hat/Turtle funnel has enough
   quantitative ablation data for a reproducible benchmark note. Do not run
   missing ablations until the note's novelty and comparison set are defined.
4. Only after one package is closed should candidate generation resume, and
   then outside the classified polykite catalog: for example a precisely
   defined poly-`K`, Sturmian-encoding, or new-substrate family motivated by a
   theorem rather than raw enumeration.
