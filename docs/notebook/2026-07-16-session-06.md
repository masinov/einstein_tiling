# Session 06 — 2026-07-16 — Wider E4 reference and robustness gate

## Done

   reference patches with projected centered-hypercube acceptance windows.
2. Extended A4 symmetry voting to 8- and 10-fold peak sets and added finite
   narrow-peak spectral-mass diagnostics.
3. Added patch-size doubling and deliberately rotated/sheared rank-recovery

4. Ran 10,000 randomized exact Bravais-lattice periodic controls with a

5. Added a Taylor–Socolar hierarchy reference. It detects five consecutive

6. Implemented stochastic square–triangle boundary growth from the published

   control, as structure factors for random tilings are ensemble quantities.

   and four new spectrum images.

## Verified / evidence

- Penrose: rank 4 and 10-fold at 561 and 1,307 vertices.
- Ammann–Beenker: rank 4 and 8-fold at 869 and 2,617 vertices.
- Both retain rank 4 after rotation and shear; shear correctly destroys the
  Euclidean rotational vote without changing module rank.
- Taylor–Socolar: 59,407 sites across seven 2-adic classes, measured dyadic
  depth 5; class-erased depth 1.
- Random square–triangle samples are valid edge-to-edge triangle/square
  patches, require no accepted overlaps, and reproduce a triangle:square
  ratio near the published 2.3 without fixing the final count.
- Periodic controls: 10,000 total; coarse ranks 2/3/4 =
  9,842/149/9; all nine coarse rank-4 cases reject at confirmation
  resolution, so the confirmed false-positive rate is 0.
- Reproducible artifacts:
  `docs/notebook/assets/e4-wide-results.json` and
  `e4-spectrum-{penrose,ammann-beenker,taylor-socolar,square-triangle-random}.png`.
- Final regressions: **50 fast passed, 12 deselected** in 5.65 s;
  **12 slow passed, 50 deselected** in 79.45 s.

## Failures and corrections

1. The first 10,000-control process had loaded an older script before the
   Taylor–Socolar edits landed, so its JSON omitted that reference. It was
   treated as stale and rerun from a clean process.
2. Nine periodic controls falsely reached rank 4 on the coarse 192² screen.
   Increasing arbitrary coefficient bounds did not solve the numerical
   issue reliably. A fixed 256² confirmation rejected all nine; this
   two-resolution rule is now part of D-0011.
3. A single random square–triangle realization was naively classified as a
   rank-4/twelvefold quasicrystal candidate. That is the intended blind-spot
   test: its vertices lie on a rank-4 substrate and have broad dodecagonal
   peaks. Added ensemble power and background-subtracted *narrow*-peak mass,
   which separates diffuse ordered maxima from Bragg peaks.
4. The first boundary grower retried only the nearest concavity and could
   dead-end on a curved interface. Scanning candidates in radial order for
   the nearest collision-free legal move removed the stalls and overlap
   rejections.
