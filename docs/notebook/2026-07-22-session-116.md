**Mode:** on-paper contact theorem; no experiment

## Proposition

Give a finite sufficient condition under which an N26 auxiliary side belongs
to one uniquely recoverable full-shield docking rather than an uncontrolled
alternative contact.

## Result

K11S uses one atomic root side. Vertex alignment turns every gapless cover of
that side into a finite word of complete intrinsic sides. If the complete
cover table contains only one full mate, its endpoint alignment and opposite
interior half-plane fix the neighbor's Euclidean pose. An exact disjointness
and full-arc intersection check then forces every duplicated side on the arc
to belong to that same shield pair.

This proves that auxiliary duplication is not intrinsically uncontrollable.
The certificate is bounded and smaller than a contact atlas: one side-cover
table, one mate identity, and one exact two-copy intersection proof. Sliding
is not assumed away; vertex alignment is an explicit obligation.

## Outcome decision

The theorem survives. K10B has one natural root, its unique `H=7` side, but
single-side uniqueness does not exclude a subdivided T-junction cover.
Session 117 must test that root against the actual K10B length roles and then
fire HC-20's decision rule. No coordinate work is reopened by K11S alone.

## Verification

- A root cover has at most `floor(|r|/lambda)` full-side entries, where
  `lambda` is the positive shortest side, once vertex alignment is proved.
- A rooted segment and the chosen interior half-plane fix a Euclidean polygon
  placement.
- The conclusion remains explicitly conditional on exact full-arc
  intersection and interior disjointness.
