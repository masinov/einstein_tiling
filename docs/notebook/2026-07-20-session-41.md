# Session 41 — primary-source horizon and family correction

## Trigger

The user supplied a literature synthesis showing that the previous Turtle
identity correction was too narrow. The program had not treated the complete
`Tile(a,b)` classification, the published exhaustive polykite horizon, or the
alignment appendix as controlling prior art. This made the E2 discovery design
and W4 dependency graph unsound.

## Primary-source audit

The controlling peer-reviewed Hat paper was checked directly, not through a
summary:

- Section 6 defines Hat as `Tile(1,sqrt(3))` and Turtle as
  `Tile(sqrt(3),1)`.
- Theorem 6.1 gives the combinatorial equivalence across positive parameters
  other than the equilateral exception. Together with the Hat theorem it
  classifies every positive unequal member as aperiodic.
- `Tile(a,a)`, `Tile(a,0)`, and `Tile(0,b)` admit periodic tilings.
- `Tile(1,k sqrt(3))` and `Tile(k sqrt(3),1)` are polykites for every
  positive odd integer `k`.
- The paper explicitly reports no other aperiodic `n`-kites for **`n≤24`**.
  The value 21 in the supplied synthesis is not the value in the primary
  paper.
- Appendix A, Lemmas A.1/A.3/A.5, proves that periodic polykite tileability
  can be reduced to aligned periodic tileability. Lemma A.6 proves the
  stronger all-tilings alignment statement for the Hat.

The peer-reviewed chiral paper was also checked directly. `Tile(1,1)` has a
mixed-handed periodic tiling but is aperiodic when only orientation-preserving
motions are allowed. Lemma 2.1 constructs families of strict Spectres from
suitable smooth edge paths; the authors explicitly do not characterize all
Spectres and leave some path-regularity cases open.

Kaplan's 2025 review reports a subsequent search of about 500 billion
polykites with no unusual behavior beyond Hat/Turtle. This is recorded as an
author-review report, not elevated to a machine-certified classification.
A dated search through current 2025--2026 literature found work on alternative
proofs, dynamics, diffraction, physical realizations, applications, and group
monotiles, but no published proof of a combinatorially independent connected
unmarked Euclidean-plane family. Absence from this targeted search is not a
theorem.

## Corrections

1. ERR-004 supersedes the frozen E2 claim that `n≈22--24` lies beyond known
   work. E2 is invalidated before launch.
2. D-0049 installs `docs/literature/POLYKITE_BASELINE.md` as the mandatory
   prior-art claim matrix.
3. The code now separates three concepts:
   - exact named-key registration;
   - membership in the published finite search horizon;
   - eligibility for an aperiodic-discovery claim.
4. Promotion is fail-closed. Every `n≤24` polykite is ineligible. Above 24,
   an unregistered key remains blocked until an exact `Tile(a,b)` family audit
   rejects membership.
5. W4 is removed from the critical periodicity path. It remains optional for
   the stronger assertion that every tiling shares one grid, or for extensions
   to substrates outside Appendix A.

## Interpretation of existing work

No exact certificate is deleted or weakened. E1 through `n≤16` is a useful
blind validation and labeled-corpus experiment, but could not have produced a
new aperiodic polykite under the published `n≤24` result. Turtle W1--W3 work
remains independent certificate-method development on a known control. It
must not be marketed as discovery or as filling a literature gap already
closed by Appendix A.
