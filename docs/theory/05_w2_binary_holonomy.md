# W2.C binary families and W2.D torus holonomy

## T2.C4 — composing period-vector and quotient certificates

Let an HNF lattice be

\[
L(a,b,d)=\langle(a,0),(b,d)\rangle,
\qquad a,d>0,\quad0\le b<a.
\]

For a vector (v=(x,y)), exact membership is

\[
v\in L(a,b,d)
\iff d\mid y\ \text{and}\ a\mid x-(y/d)b.
\]

> **T2.C4 (binary period-family composition).** If no grid-aligned tiling by
> a tile (T) has period (v), then no quotient lattice (L) containing (v)
> admits an exact cover by (T).

*Proof.* An exact cover of the quotient lifts to an (L)-periodic plane tiling.
Every element of (L), in particular (v), is then a period, contradicting the
hypothesis. ∎

This is a binary certificate: the no-period premise is supplied by W1's
complete exact-cover transfer graph, not by a linear relaxation. The compact
quotient certificate stores `(a,b,d)`, the excluded vector, and its two HNF
generator coefficients. Its verifier checks the displayed divisibilities and
reconstructs the vector exactly; T1.2-36 is an explicit theorem dependency.

For the finalist, the 126 independently certified vectors with
(0<Q(v)\le36) define 126 infinite congruence families. Exact HNF enumeration
shows that every quotient lattice of index at most 36 contains one of these
vectors. Index 37 is the first incomplete shell, with precisely HNFs
`(37,10,1)` and `(37,26,1)` outside this certificate class. Across the
area-admissible HNFs through index 215, the families kill 2,941 of 8,864;
the remaining 5,923 are unknown to this certificate, not tileable.

Artifact: `docs/notebook/assets/theory-w2-binary-families.json`.

## T2.C5 — all three thin directions

T2.C1 proved the GF(2) obstruction for `(1,0,k)`, all (k\ge4). The exact
center-lattice D6 action sends this family to the other two observed thin
directions:

- operation 8 sends it to `(k,0,1)`;
- operation 2 sends it to `(k,k-1,1)`.

The producer records the action matrices and images of both lattice
generators; the verifier derives those matrices again from the substrate's
geometric `transform_cell` action.

> **T2.C5 (thin-orbit obstruction).** For every (k\ge4), none of the HNFs
> `(1,0,k)`, `(k,0,1)`, or `(k,k-1,1)` admits a finalist exact cover.

This closes the symmetry statement that had previously only been observed in
the finite 36/742 GF(2) table.

## D0 — primary-source planar boundary invariant

Conway and Lagarias define, for square-lattice direction group
(F=\langle A,U\rangle), the normal subgroup generated by tile-boundary words.
Their Theorem 2.1 states that a simply connected tileable region's boundary
word must lie in that subgroup. In their three-in-line control they use

\[
H=\langle\!\langle A^3,U^3,(U^{-1}A)^3\rangle\!\rangle
\]

whose quotient has the planar (p3) triangle/hexagon Cayley diagram. Total
winding about the (U)-labelled triangular faces vanishes on the three legal
tile boundaries, while the staircase boundary has value
(\lfloor(N+1)/3\rfloor). This proves their Theorem 1.2.

The implementation realizes (p3) exactly by Eisenstein-affine transformations,
traces words in the Cayley diagram, and computes winding using rational
orientation tests. It reproduces all three zero tile values and the staircase
formula for the 41 applicable values through (N=60). Primary source:
[Conway--Lagarias, JCTA 53 (1990), 183--208](https://doi.org/10.1016/0097-3165(90)90057-4).

## T2.D1 — the torus boundary-skeleton lemma

Let (Q) be a finite group and let each oriented substrate-edge direction map
to an element of (Q), with reversal mapping to the inverse. Assume every
allowed oriented tile-boundary word maps to the identity. For an actual plane
tiling, let (B) be the union of tile boundaries.

> **T2.D1 (necessary commuting holonomies; proof draft).** If the tile-boundary
> skeleton (B) is connected and the tiling has independent periods
> (\ell_1,\ell_2), choose boundary paths from a vertex (p) to
> (p+\ell_1) and (p+\ell_2). Their labels (h_1,h_2\in Q) commute.

*Proof draft.* Translate the two paths and concatenate them around the period
parallelogram. In the simply connected lifted tiled plane this closed
boundary-skeleton path is a product of conjugates of whole tile boundaries,
so its label is the identity. That label is
([h_1,h_2]). Connectivity supplies the paths. For edge-to-edge polykite exact
covers, connectivity follows from connectivity of the substrate cell-adjacency
graph and of each disk-like tile boundary. ∎

The qualification “boundary paths” is essential. A canonical substrate path
may cross tile interiors; its label is not invariant modulo whole-tile
boundary relators. Therefore a displacement-only commutator is not yet a
sound torus obstruction.

## Phase-0 finalist quotient search and no-go

The finalist has six unoriented kite-edge directions and twelve distinct D6
tile-boundary relators. Exhaustive enumeration of all (6^6=46,656) generator
maps into (S_3) finds 3,474 homomorphisms, including 2,556 surjections.
Thus genuinely nonabelian finite targets exist abundantly.

For each surjection, however, the image of the zero-displacement free-word
kernel has order 6 in 2,322 cases and order 3 (the normal subgroup (A_3)) in
234 cases. No surjection has trivial displacement kernel. Moreover every pair
of displacement cosets has commuting representatives. Consequently an
(S_3) test that remembers displacement but not the chosen binary boundary
network cannot obstruct any torus quotient.

This is a useful no-go, not a Layer-D failure. The next exact certificate must
jointly encode:

1. binary placement selection and exact cell cover;
2. which quotient edges belong to selected tile boundaries;
3. finite-group potentials on that connected boundary graph;
4. two commuting twisted boundary conditions for the torus generators.

An UNSAT result from that coupled CSP can become a nonabelian quotient
certificate. The uncoupled commutator route is retired. Artifact:
`docs/notebook/assets/theory-w2-layer-d-phase0.json`.

## T2.D2 — the binary-coupled finite-group obstruction

Fix a homomorphism from the six-direction free edge group to a finite group
`Q`, with reversal sent to inverse, and suppose every allowed oriented tile
boundary maps to the identity. For an HNF lattice `L`, the coupled CSP has:

1. one Boolean variable per legal quotient placement;
2. an at-least-one placement clause for every quotient cell (overlap is
   deliberately allowed);
3. one `Q`-valued potential at every quotient substrate vertex;
4. a selected-placement implication for each of its boundary edges; and
5. a fixed commuting pair `(h1,h2)` giving the two twisted deck conditions.

The potential convention is

\[
P(y)=P(x)\rho(e),\qquad P(x+\ell)=h(\ell)P(x),
\]

so a quotient edge whose lifted endpoints have deck coordinates `s,t` imposes

\[
g_y=h(t)^{-1}h(s)g_x\rho(e).
\]

> **T2.D2 (binary-coupled torus obstruction; proof draft).** If this CSP is
> UNSAT for every commuting pair in `Q²`, then the tile has no exact cover of
> the `L`-torus.

*Proof.* An exact cover is a permitted at-least-one selection. In the plane,
the union of tile boundaries is connected: contracting each connected tile
in the connected kite-cell adjacency graph leaves a connected tile-adjacency
graph, and adjacent tiles share boundary. Every finite boundary-skeleton loop
is a product of conjugates of tile boundaries, so its image in `Q` is the
identity. Path labels therefore define a developing potential. Translating a
base path by the two periods supplies `h1,h2`; the period parallelogram gives
their commutator, hence they commute. Reducing the potential modulo `L` gives
exactly the CSP equations. Thus every exact torus cover yields a model of at
least one commuting-twist CSP. Contraposition proves the claim. ∎

Allowing overlaps is important to the certificate polarity. It weakens exact
cover, so an UNSAT result cannot be an accidental restatement of the ordinary
exact-cover search. A SAT model is only a failure of this obstruction; it is
not a tiling.

## T2.D3 — exact D6 covariance and symmetry reduction

Every point-group operation permutes the six signed kite-edge generators.
If `g` is in `D6`, it sends a period lattice `L` covariantly to `gL` and a
boundary quotient map `rho` contravariantly to `rho o g^-1`. The latter is
then reduced modulo simultaneous inner conjugacy in `S3`.

> **T2.D3 (D6 covariance; proof draft, exact implementation).** The Layer-D
> CSP for `(L,rho)` and any commuting twist pair is isomorphic, after the
> corresponding relabeling of twists, to the CSP for
> `(gL,rho o g^-1)`. Consequently SAT/UNSAT over all commuting twists is
> constant on diagonal `D6` orbits of HNF/map pairs.

*Proof.* A kite-grid symmetry bijects quotient cells, legal placements,
boundary edges and quotient vertices. Its exact signed permutation of the six
edge directions carries every selected-placement implication to the
corresponding transformed implication. Transport the developing potential
along the vertex bijection and express the two deck transformations in the
transformed HNF basis; this bijects commuting twist pairs because an integral
change of period generators preserves commutation. All clauses are therefore
carried bijectively, and the inverse point-group operation supplies the inverse
model map. Inner conjugation merely relabels all `S3` values. ∎

At index 45, the nine frontier HNFs are exactly two `D6` orbits, of sizes
three and six. The 39 strong quotient classes form seven orbits of sizes
`3,3,6,3,6,6,12`; the nine effective maps are exactly two complete orbits,
`{9,21,30}` and `{15,18,24,27,33,36}`. The diagonal action partitions the
351 matrix entries into 43 orbits (three of size 3, 23 of size 6, and 17 of
size 12). All 4,212 entry/operation comparisons agree exactly. This accounts
for the three repeated column signatures without asserting that the displayed
same-signature triples are themselves group orbits.

Implementation and certificate:
`src/einstein/theory/holonomy_symmetry.py` and
`theory-w2-layer-d-symmetry.json`.

## D-W2-D4 — index-50 saturation of the strong S3 target

The 18 index-50 HNFs surviving T1.2-36 form three six-element `D6` orbits.
Crossing them with all 39 strong maps would require 702 scans; T2.D3 reduces
the complete logical matrix to 81 diagonal-orbit representatives. Exactly
four representatives are holonomy-obstructed. Their expansions give 36
killing HNF/map pairs and exclude precisely one HNF orbit:

`(25,9,2)`, `(25,14,2)`, `(50,18,1)`, `(50,21,1)`, `(50,28,1)`,
`(50,31,1)`.

The other two HNF orbits—12 HNFs—survive every strong S3 map. Therefore this
finite-group target does not close the index-50 shell, and at this S3-only
stage the complete machine-verified prefix remained 45. This negative result is useful: extending
Layer D now requires strengthening the quotient target or extracting a new
constraint from the surviving relaxed models, not merely evaluating more S3
maps of the same boundary presentation.

All six positive exclusions have now passed the independent proof pipeline:
108/108 selected map/twist cores verify both during production and in a cold
standalone replay. The compressed certificate payload is 283,140,121 bytes.
Conversely, all 77 non-obstructed pair-orbit representatives have explicit
canonical-CNF truth assignments; a separate verifier regenerates each CNF and
checks every clause. These are witnesses only for the relaxed Layer-D CSP,
not exact covers.

Artifacts: `theory-w2-layer-d-s3-index50.json`,
`theory-w2-layer-d-proof-index50.json`, and
`theory-w2-layer-d-sat-index50.json`.

Decoding the 77 relaxed assignments shows that all active boundary networks
are connected. The missing condition is overlap: every model uses more than
the exact-cover count of 30 placements, with coverage surplus at least 80.
Twenty-four witnesses have maximum cell multiplicity two. Thus connectivity
constraints would add nothing on these witnesses; the next controlled
relaxation caps multiplicity at two while still containing every exact cover.

That complete bounded-overlap scan has the identical polarity matrix: four
UNSAT and 77 SAT diagonal pair orbits, hence the same six excluded and twelve
surviving HNFs. All 77 SAT results have clause-level witnesses independently
checked against regenerated CNFs. Coverage multiplicity above two is therefore
not the source of saturation. Artifacts:
`theory-w2-layer-d-overlap2-index50.json` and
`theory-w2-layer-d-overlap2-sat-index50.json`.

## D-W2-D6 — complementary A4 closure of index 50

An exact small-target census was used to choose the next quotient rather than
guessing one. Besides reproducing the S3 control, it found 1,824 proper-kernel
inner classes for each of D4 and Q8, but only 48 for A4. Every strong A4 class
has zero-displacement kernel `V4`, so its residual quotient is
`A4/V4 = C3`, complementary to the `S3/A3 = C2` information retained by the
strong S3 classes.

Crossing the 12 S3-surviving HNFs with the 48 strong A4 classes gives 576
logical pairs. Exact T2.D3 covariance reduces this to 48 diagonal D6 pair
orbits. Sixteen representatives are UNSAT for every one of the 48 commuting
A4 twist pairs; their orbit expansions obstruct all 12 HNFs. The other 32
representatives have explicit truth assignments which pass a cold
clause-by-clause check. As always, those assignments witness only the relaxed
at-least-cover CSP, not tilings.

The 16 killing columns are identical for all 12 HNFs and admit an exact finite
description: the last three boundary-generator images are three distinct
elements of `V4`. Equivalently their induced C3 signatures are the inverse
pair `(1,2,1,0,0,0)` and `(2,1,2,0,0,0)`. They form two complete D6 map orbits,
of sizes 12 and four. This signature is a finite classification and a target
for a future symbolic HNF-family theorem; it is not itself such a theorem.

For proof promotion, the deterministic lowest killing class, map 7, was fixed
on each HNF. All 12 × 48 direct twist CNFs produced Glucose 4 DRAT proofs.
`drat-trim` independently verified every raw proof, extracted and reverified a
core, and the standalone cold audit then regenerated every canonical CNF,

replayed all 576 stored proofs. The compressed certificate payload is
2,166,298,658 bytes.

> **T2.D2-50 (machine-verified finite theorem).** No grid-aligned finalist
