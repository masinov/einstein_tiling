# Session 20 — theory-track adoption and overnight recovery

**Date:** 2026-07-17  
**Scope:** recover the interrupted finalist campaign; adopt and structure the
theorem-producing research track.

## Starting point

The finalist had strong finite evidence—nested cores, a robust rank-4 A4
signal, and many exact finite quotient refutations—but no proof of an infinite
tiling or of aperiodicity.  The power outage terminated every overnight
`screen` session.  It therefore also terminated any job that had not already
written a completion line.

The user chose a deliberate theory detour: develop exact tools capable of
settling plane tileability and periodicity rather than interpreting ever larger
disk patches as a proof.

## Recovered overnight evidence

The append-only logs were normalized by
`scripts/recover_e1_overnight.py`.  The resulting checksummed artifact is
[`e1-overnight-recovered.json`](assets/e1-overnight-recovered.json).

- The generic campaign completed 19 determinant indices.  These contain 9,099
  HNF quotient instances in total.
- The targeted campaigns completed 11 period-47 cylinders and 25 return-scale
  lattice quotients.
- All 9,135 explicitly completed quotient executions reported exact UNSAT.  No
  periodic certificate was found.  These are execution counts rather than a
  claim of 9,135 cases disjoint from earlier campaigns; several determinant
  indices were deliberate reruns.
- This is not an aperiodicity proof.  The campaign was interrupted, and every
  planned or started job without a completion line remains unknown.
- Reported per-job times are concurrent process runtimes.  They must not be
  summed and compared with wall-clock overnight duration.

The hierarchy screen did finish before the outage.  It tested 22,094 proposed
first-composition rules: 22,066 failed coverage, 26 failed SAT, and two related
arity-8 deletion rules survived.  Neither is uniquely composable on the six
patch samples.  Broader 6–12-child single-arity libraries did not fit all
samples; the unrestricted 4–12 recursive feasibility probe was satisfiable but
forced zero inner groups.  Thus this is a non-recognizable first-composition
hint, not a substitution certificate.

## Theory program adopted

The adopted roadmap is
[`theory_research_plan.md`](../program/theory_research_plan.md), version 0.2.
Its foundational correction is the periodic-completion theorem: for a finite
grid-aligned tile on this periodic substrate, existence of any singly periodic
tiling implies existence of a doubly periodic tiling.  Consequently the four
separate periodicity obligations collapse to one exact obligation inside the
grid-aligned scope.

The theorem has been separated from the roadmap as
[`01_periodic_completion.md`](../theory/01_periodic_completion.md).  It is
currently a **proof-draft**, pending an independent proof audit and literature
check; it is not silently promoted to theorem-ready status.

## Decisions and next gate

Decision D-0027 adopts the theory track and its governance.  The first
implementation gate is W1.a: a pure-Python, exact higher-block transfer engine
validated against periodic controls, the hat, and existing A1 certificates.
It must enumerate arbitrary nonzero period vectors (including nonprimitive
ones), reconstruct positive certificates, and produce an auditable exhaustion
witness for negative decisions.  A search seeded only from an empty frontier
is prohibited because it can miss closed recurrent components.

No finalist conclusion changed in this session: it remains a prioritized
finite-data candidate, with both plane tileability and aperiodicity unresolved.

## W1.a reference implementation begun

`src/einstein/theory/transfer.py` now implements the exact small-cylinder
reference automaton.  For a period vector `v=g p`, it chooses an exact
unimodular transverse vector, retains the `g` torsion positions, compiles whole
tile placements by transverse span, and enumerates all nonoverlapping unions
of contributions crossing a cut.  It then fills one column exactly and searches
the entire graph—not only an empty-frontier component—for a directed cycle.

Every discovered cycle is converted to an HNF torus exact-cover certificate
and checked by the independent A1 verifier.  Initial tests pass for primitive
and nonprimitive periodic controls, the unique Myers-validated n=2 non-tiler,
and a four-kite control with period `(2,0)` but not `(1,0)`.  The last control
guards against accidentally replacing arbitrary vectors by primitive ones.
Resource limits have the explicit verdict `resource-exhausted`.

An exploratory hat check is cycle-free for `(1,0)`, `(0,1)`, `(1,1)` and
`(2,0)`.

The exploratory checks were then promoted to a reproducible finite validation
artifact by `scripts/run_theory_w1_controls.py`.  The matrix contains all 28
combinations of the seven free n≤3 polykites with the four control vectors,
plus the torsion trap and hat controls.  Across 102 independently solved small
transverse tori it records 25 A1-verified transfer cycles, zero disagreements
and zero resource exhaustions.  Artifact:
[`theory-w1-phase0-controls.json`](assets/theory-w1-phase0-controls.json).

This is not a new hat theorem or a closed W1.a gate.  Cycle-free graph hashes
are reproducibility fingerprints, not standalone completeness proof objects;
complete adjacency/exhaustion manifests and an independent negative verifier
remain required.
